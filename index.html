01 · The Market

What is currency derivatives trading?

Currency derivatives are contracts whose value derives from exchange rates: forwards, non-deliverable forwards (NDFs), FX swaps, options and listed futures. Corporations and asset managers use them to hedge; central banks and funds use them to manage reserves; and traders — discretionary and systematic — take directional, relative-value and volatility positions. FX has no single "spot close," no listing regime and no cash-flow anchor like equities: prices emerge from interest-rate differentials, macro flows and positioning, which makes it one of the most quantitatively driven markets there is.

💱G10 Majors

EURUSD, USDJPY, GBPUSD, AUDUSD, USDCAD, USDCHF and the NZD pair. Deepest liquidity, tightest spreads, exchange-listed options (CME/PHlx) plus massive OTC books. The training ground for every FX quant.

🌏Emerging-Market Crosses

USDBRL, USDINR, USDCNH, USDZAR, USDTRY, MXN, KRW. Often restricted or partially convertible — traded as NDFs with offshore fixings. Higher vol, fatter tails, richer carry: the alpha-and-risk frontier of FX.

⚙️Crosses & Exotics

EURGBP, EURJPY, AUDNZD derived via triangular arbitrage off USD legs; plus barrier options, digitals, targeted-knock-outs and accumulators sold by structuring desks to corporates. Quants price and hedge the path-dependence.

Why quants fit here: FX returns are driven by observable, modelable primitives — rate differentials (covered interest parity), macro data releases, order flow and volatility surfaces. Volatility itself is the P&L: realized vs implied gaps, risk-reversal skew and term structure are tradable on any horizon from milliseconds to months. That breadth — macro research, stat-arb, vol trading, HFT market-making — is why FX desks hire more quants per trader than almost any other asset class.
02 · The Instrument Shelf

Core currency derivative products

Know what each product is for, who issues it, and where the quant edge lives.

➡️FX Forward

OTC agreement to exchange two currencies at a set rate on a future date. Priced by interest-rate differentials: F = S·e^{(r_d−r_f)T}. The building block of all FX curve math and corporate hedging.

🚫Non-Deliverable Forward (NDF)

Cash-settled forward for restricted currencies (INR, BRL, KRW, CNH…). Settles fixing-vs-contract difference in USD. Its premium/discount encodes capital controls and offshore demand — a signal quants model directly.

🔄FX Swap & CCS

Simultaneous buy/sell of a currency pair at two dates (spot-forward or forward-forward) — the funding engine of global banks. Cross-currency swaps expose the basis between currencies and underpin covered-interest-parity deviations.

📈FX Option

Right, not obligation, to exchange at a strike (European vanilla). Traded OTC (interbank) and listed (CME/PHlx). Quoted in Black-Scholes implied vol; the smile/skew carries the risk-reversal and butterfly information desks trade.

🎯Exotics & Structured Payoffs

Barriers (knock-in/out), digitals, Asian (average-rate) options, targeted knock-outs, accumulators/deccies. Sold to corporates via structuring; hedged by quant desks running Monte Carlo and local-vol/stochastic-vol models.

🏛️Listed FX Futures & Options

Exchange-cleared futures on CME, SGX, MCX, DCE… and clearing migrating OTC swaps toward CCPs post-Dodd-Frank/EMIR. Transparent margins and curves — the accessible entry point for independent systematic traders.

03 · Speak The Language

Industry terminology decoder

Filter by category. Every FX desk interview assumes you know these cold.

Currency pair & base/quote currency Conventions

Every FX price is relative: EURUSD 1.08 means 1 euro buys 1.08 US dollars — EUR is the base, USD the quote. You always buy the base and sell the quote. Convention order (EUR>USD>GBP>AUD…) matters when coding tickers correctly.

Pip Conventions

"Percentage in point" — the 4th decimal for most pairs (0.0001), 2nd for JPY pairs (0.01). Quoting convention: dealers quote "points" above spot (e.g., "two-five" = 25 points = 0.0025). Position sizing starts from pip value.

Bid / offer / spread Conventions

The dealer's buy (bid) and sell (offer) prices; the gap is the spread — your round-trip cost. In liquid majors it can be sub-half-pip; in EM crosses several pips. Microstructure quants model spread dynamics and adverse selection explicitly.

TOM / SPOT / TOM-next Conventions

Standard settlement dates: today (T0), tomorrow (TOM/T1) and spot (T2, or T1 for some pairs). Rolling off the short-dated book nightly ("tom-next") is where funding views become trades — and where LIBOR-era swap-point math still lives.

ON / TN / SN / FN Conventions

Overnight, Tom-Next, Spot-Next, Forward-Next — the tenor ladder of FX swap points. Each tenor has its own supply/demand (month-end, quarter-end flows) and its own microstructure signature.

Big figure / dealing room slang Conventions

Traders quote only the last digits once the big figure is known ("deal with me at two-five"). Other classics: "cable" (GBPUSD), "fiber" (EURUSD), "kiwi", "loonie", "the greenback". Knowing the slang signals desk readiness.

ECN / EBS / RFQ Conventions

Electronic communication networks (EBS, Reuters Matching, LMAX) and request-for-quote streams replaced open voice outcry. Today most interbank volume is algorithmic RFQ — latency, queue position and fill probability are quant problems.

Interest-rate differential / swap points Pricing

Forward rates deviate from spot by the two currencies' rate difference: F ≈ S·(1+r_d·T)/(1+r_f·T). The deviation quoted in "swap points" is the purest visible price of money carry in FX.

Covered interest parity (CIP) & cross-currency basis Pricing

No-arbitrage link between spot, forward and the two risk-free rates. Post-2008 the basis persistently deviates from zero — bank balance-sheet constraints, Fed dollar-swap lines and regulation make CIP violations a tradable funding signal.

NDF fixing & deliverable premium Pricing

NDFs settle against an official fixing (e.g., RBI INR reference, WM/Refinitiv London 4pm close). When onshore/offshore convertibility differs, NDF pricing embeds a "deliverable premium" — a direct read on capital-control pressure.

Triangular arbitrage / cross derivation Pricing

EURGBP must equal EURUSD ÷ GBPEUR (with bid/offer orientation flipped correctly). Violations beyond transaction cost are arbitraged in microseconds; modeling the three-leg execution race is classic FX HFT.

WM/Refinitiv & ESMA closes Pricing

The 4pm London benchmark auction used by mutual funds and index providers; Europe's mandated closing-window fixes. Massive predictable flows concentrate there — window internalization and impact estimation are real quant businesses.

Forward curve & roll Pricing

The term structure of swap points across tenors. Shifting rate expectations reprice the whole curve; rolling a forward book monthly realizes curve changes — FX's analogue of commodity roll yield.

Implied volatility (IV) & the smile Options

The vol that reproduces market option prices under Black-76. FX smiles are pronounced: deep OTM puts (crash fear) and OTM calls (devaluation fear) both price above ATM. Smile level and slope are themselves traded as vol products.

Risk reversal (RR) Options

Difference between OTM call and OTM put implied vols — the market's directional skew. RRs blow out before crises (e.g., EUR calls vs puts in 2010, EM puts pre-shock) and are a leading sentiment gauge quants backtest heavily.

Butterfly / strangle Options

Butterfly measures curvature (wing premium over the RR mid); strangles combine symmetric wings. Decomposing an FX option surface into ATM vol + RR + BF isolates exactly which part of the smile you're betting on.

Delta, gamma, vega, charm, vanna Options

First-order Greeks plus second-order cross-effects: charm = delta decay with time, vanna = delta change with vol. Dealer hedging of vanna/charm drives "gamma squeezes" and pinning around large strikes — a live FX phenomenon.

Vol-of-vol & stochastic vol Options

How fast implied vol itself moves. Modeled by SABR/Heston; critical for pricing long-dated FX options and for anyone running a vol book where variance, not direction, is the underlying.

Barrier levels & knockout zones Options

Knock-in/knock-out strikes where hedging flows step-change as barriers trigger. Desks model "barrier danger zones" probabilistically; breaching one can cascade spot moves — path-dependence made visible.

Correlation (FX vs assets) Options

EURUSD vs equity vol, commodity FX (AUD, NOK, RUB) vs their underlying metals/oil, MXN vs oil. Multi-asset correlation regimes decide cross-asset books; correlation swaps monetize them directly.

Notional vs margin Risk

FX trades huge notionals on tiny margins (retail leverage up to 30:1–500:1; institutional CCP margins ~1–5%). Real risk is tail-move × notional — sizing must come from VaR/stress, never from available leverage.

VaR / CVaR & stress testing Risk

Worst-loss-at-confidence metrics plus scenario replay (SNB CHF floor removal −30% in minutes, 2016 Brexit gap, TRY devaluations). FX gaps through stops — fat-tailed, jump-aware risk models are mandatory, not optional.

CSA, netting & XVA Risk

Collateral agreements (CSAs) and netting sets reduce counterparty exposure post-trade; XVA charges (CVA, FVA, KVA) price credit, funding and capital costs into every OTC deal. Modern FX desks quote with XVA engines in the loop.

Funding / carry & rollover cost Risk

Holding a long-EURUSD position means borrowing USD against EUR collateral daily — the tom-next rate is your true carry. Carry regimes flip with central-bank policy; the entire "carry trade" factor is this mechanic scaled up.

Position limits & reporting Risk

Regulatory and internal caps on net open exposure per currency/tenor; MiFID II/CFTC rules push cleared flow to CCPs. Systematic books must bake limit checks and best-execution analytics into the pipeline.

Carry trade Macro

Long high-yield, short low-yield currency, harvesting the rate differential (historically JPY-funded, now USD-long vs low-rate pairs). High Sharpe until it isn't — crash-prone, so quants overlay vol targeting and drawdown control.

Momentum & time-series trend Macro

Currencies that appreciated over 1–12 months tend to keep appreciating (weight-of-evidence across decades of data). Combined with carry and value, momentum forms the canonical FX factor trio.

PPP & real-effective-rate value Macro

Purchasing-power parity says exchange rates drift toward relative price levels; REER indices operationalize it. Slow, powerful at 5-year horizons — the fundamental anchor for long-horizon quant macro books.

Macro announcement effects Macro

NFP, CPI, central-bank decisions produce the largest intraday FX moves. Event studies around release windows — surprise magnitude, hawkish/dovish scoring of statements via NLP — are standard quant research projects.

Order flow & microstructure alpha Quant

Toxicity of flow, queue dynamics on RFQ streams, footprint of benchmark auctions. Market-maker P&L comes from inventory management under informed/uninformed flow — a genuinely statistical control problem.

Regime switching & vol targeting Quant

FX alternates between trending, mean-reverting and crisis regimes detectable via HMMs, realized-vol clustering and cross-asset stress gauges. Position sizing keyed to regime/vol is what separates surviving systematic books from blown ones.

Backtesting pitfalls in FX Quant

No single consolidated tape, bid/offer asymmetry, weekend gaps, holiday calendars, changing conventions (LIBOR→SOFR). A credible FX backtest needs tick-level bid/offer data — close-price-only tests systematically overstate performance.

Key skills required

FX quants sit at the intersection of stochastic calculus, macro intuition and electronic-market engineering. Roughly what hiring managers weight:

Machine learning (gradient boosting, deep nets, NLP on central-bank text)High
Optimization & portfolio construction (factor allocation, vol targeting)High
Python (pandas, numpy, statsmodels) — research & productionMust-have
C++ / Rust — pricing libraries & low-latency executionStrong plus
SQL + data engineering (tick bid/offer, curves, surfaces)Must-have
Domain sense: rate differentials, macro calendar, clearing & fundingDifferentiator

🧮Mathematics

Linear algebra, Itô calculus, jump-diffusions (FX gaps!), OU processes for spreads, cointegration/Kalman filters for crosses, Monte Carlo for exotics, convex optimization for book construction.

💻Engineering

Event-driven backtesters handling bid/offer and holiday calendars, kdb+/ClickHouse tick stores, FIX-API connectivity to ECNs/brokers, cloud pipelines, and enough networking to reason about colocation and RFQ latency.

🗣️Communication & grit

Explaining a vol-surface view to PMs and clients, writing crisp trade rationales, staying disciplined through carry crashes. Desks hire people they trust under gap risk.

Certifications that help (but don't substitute for a track record): FRM (risk-heavy, very relevant to FX books), CFA (macro literacy), ACI Operations/Dealing certificates (market mechanics), and above all — a reproducible research portfolio: e.g., your own carry+momentum FX strategy backtested on real bid/offer data with honest transaction costs.

06 · How Alpha Is Made

Quant strategy families on FX desks

📈Carry & factors

Rate-differential harvesting blended with momentum and value (REER) into multi-factor portfolios; vol-targeted to survive carry crashes. The flagship systematic FX strategy.

🌍Macro / event-driven

Positioning around CPI, NFP, central-bank meetings and elections; NLP hawkishness scores; surprise-reaction studies. Discretionary-plus-quant hybrid desks.

🔀Stat-arb & relative value

Cross-consistency (triangular), forward-vs-implied fair value, NDF premium dislocations, dispersion across EM complexes. Short half-life, tightly modeled.

🎭Volatility trading

Implied-vs-realized vol, RR/BF skew expression, variance swaps, gamma scalping. Purest quant seat in FX — you trade moments, not directions.

⚡HFT & market-making

Quote-driven inventory models across ECNs, latency arbitrage between venues and the benchmark auctions, toxic-flow detection. Engineering-heavy.

🧩Structuring & client solutions

Designing exotics for corporate hedgers (TKNs, accumulators), then letting the quant desk monetize the residual flows and hedges. Sales-adjacent but deeply mathematical.

07 · The Ladder

Career path in currency derivatives

Typical progression on a systematic FX / vol desk at a bank, hedge fund or fintech market-maker.

YEARS 0–2 · ENTRY

Quant Researcher / FX Analyst

Clean data, reproduce published factors (carry/momentum/value), build signal libraries, support live books. Degrees: MFin/Math/MSc Quant; Python fluency assumed. First comp bands: $90–160k (bank), $120–200k+ (funds, NYC/London).

YEARS 2–5 · CORE

Quantitative Trader / Vol Trader

Own a strategy sleeve end-to-end: research → execution logic → risk ownership. On dealer desks, move toward options/vol quoting or electronic market-making. P&L bonuses start to dominate salary ($150–350k total).

YEARS 5–10 · SENIOR

Senior Researcher / Portfolio Manager

Run a book or a systematic FX fund strategy with allocated capital; manage juniors, interface with allocators. Comp becomes P&L share — $300k–$1M+ at good years, mark-to-market with drawdowns.

YEARS 10+ · PEAK

Desk Head / Fund Founder / Chief Quant

Own the franchise (FX & EM macro desk) or launch a vehicle (CTA-style systematic FX fund, vol boutique, fintech MM). Alternatively pivot to crypto-FX, digital-asset market making, or macro family offices.

🎯Who hires

Dealer banks (JPM, DB, Citi, UBS), systematic funds (Man/AHL, Winton, Rokos, Elliott, Two Sigma, Citadel Securities, Optiver, Flow Traders), EM specialists, central-bank reserve managers, and fintech FX (Wise payments treasury, stablecoin desks).

🚪How to break in

Competitions & credentials: Man Numeric, WorldQuant BRAIN, Rotman, StatQuest-style research write-ups. Projects that stand out: a carry-momentum-reversal replication on Dukascopy ticks; a WM-close impact study; a SABR surface-fitting library. Publish the code — GitHub counts.
